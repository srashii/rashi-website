## The problem

Botim is used every day for calls, but chat engagement lagged behind the apps people use every day, and only a small share of users ever touched its fintech services. Messaging felt functional rather than personal, and payments lived behind separate tabs, so the moments where money naturally comes up in a conversation (a birthday, a shared dinner, sending money home) were lost.

## How I approached it

### First, make chat a daily habit

- **Made chats feel like the moment.** I introduced chat themes, set per chat from the contact profile with a live preview, and festive themes like Halloween offered through an in-chat prompt. Celebration animations mark birthdays and congratulations.
- **Gave people new ways to express themselves.** I brought GIFs into chat through a rich-media integration, alongside stickers and other expressive formats.
- **Invested in the most-used media.** Voice notes are the most used media type on Botim chat, so I prioritised voice-note enhancements that make recording and listening smoother.
- **Sweated the small details.** A larger input-bar font, Reply-to on all media types and a smoother notification opt-in, backed by delivery quality improvements and a version-compatibility framework so new features degrade gracefully on older app versions.

### Then, turn conversations into money moments

- **Found the money moments in chat.** I mapped where users already talk about money and prioritised the ones with the highest intent: gifting, splitting a bill, collecting money for a group and sending money home.
- **Made the entry point part of the conversation.** A Dirham-icon tooltip, a pulsating payment call to action and contextual celebrations (birthday and congratulations animations that nudge a gift) put payments one tap away, inside the chat.
- **Brought money reminders into the chat list.** EasyCash repayments now show up as a Bill Reminder, and turn into a red Bill Overdue alert, right on the chat list, so users see what they owe where they spend their day.
- **Built one framework instead of many screens.** I designed a single transaction message-card framework that works for P2P, AANI, gifts and remittances, so every new transfer type ships as a configuration, not a rebuild.
- **Measured causally.** Every change ran as a controlled experiment with clean control groups, measured on incremental transacting users rather than raw clicks.

## Outcome

- **Messages sent per user per day grew from 18.5 to 23.8 in six months.**
- **~58k new fintech transacting users** attributable to the comms-to-fintech initiatives.
- **3% uplift in EasyCash lending repayments** after Bill Reminder and Bill Overdue alerts moved into the chat list, with 7% of users opening those messages.
- The card framework is now the shared base for new transfer types across squads.
